# LangTrainer Qt app

Open `qt-placeholder/CMakeLists.txt` in Qt Creator, select a Qt 5 or Qt 6 Desktop kit, then build and run.

The home window keeps language and practice mode choices together. Spanish has Verb conjugation, Sentence practice, and Listening and speaking. Russian and German also have Noun declension and Prepositions. German additionally has Pronoun declension and Adjective endings. Verb, noun, preposition, German pronoun, and German adjective practice are available where shown; Sentence practice and Listening and speaking remain disabled placeholders.

For verb practice, choose verbs, then tenses. Highlight a verb in the first checklist and use **View forms...** to see its stored conjugations. Russian currently has ten imperfective verbs with present, past, and compound future forms; past tense distinguishes gender where needed.

For Russian noun practice, choose nouns, then cases. Highlight a noun and use **View forms...** to see its six singular forms. Each card gives a noun and a case; type the declined noun. The starter deck contains ten nouns. For Prepositions, choose nouns, then preposition meanings. Each card shows the Russian preposition and an English meaning or context cue, but leaves the governed case for you to recall; answer with the declined noun only. The case is revealed with the answer. The deck has 36 everyday preposition meanings, including separate movement and location entries for **в**, **на**, **за**, and **под**, plus the different meanings of **с/со**. The selected nouns all begin with consonants, so **о** does not need the **об** variant. Preposition phrases are short morphology prompts; some noun combinations are unusual outside a full sentence.

German verb practice contains 61 verbs, including 20 separable verbs, across present, simple past, perfect, and future. Type the full verb form without the subject pronoun; for example, **stehe auf**, **stand auf**, **bin aufgestanden**, or **werde aufstehen**. The deck includes all eight subject categories in `spec.yaml`, including informal and formal *you*. The formal singular and plural forms are identical but remain separate card categories.

German noun practice contains 40 nouns across masculine, feminine, and neuter gender. Choose nouns, then any of the eight case and number combinations. Type the definite article and noun, preserving noun capitalization, for example **dem Mann**, **des Kindes**, or **den Büchern**. The reference window shows the full singular and plural table. German preposition practice has 38 meanings. Choose nouns, then preposition meanings; each card asks for the declined **article and noun only**. The preposition and an English context cue are shown, while the required case is revealed after the answer. Two-way prepositions have separate location and destination entries. As with the Russian drill, some noun and preposition combinations are grammar prompts rather than natural phrases. The answer format avoids contraction choices such as *im* versus *in dem*.

German pronoun practice has personal and possessive forms for each person, including informal and formal *you*. Choose pronouns, then any of the four cases. Highlight a pronoun and use **View forms...** for its table. Personal cards ask for the form in the chosen case. Possessive cards also show whether the form appears before a noun or stands alone, plus the referent's gender or plural number; type only the declined pronoun. The deck includes nominative, accusative, dative, and genitive, although genitive personal forms are uncommon in conversation. Formal *Sie* is one selection for singular and plural because its forms coincide. Accepted variants are shown in the reference table and feedback.

German adjective practice contains 38 common adjectives. Choose adjectives, then select article patterns (definite, *ein/kein/possessive*, or no article), cases, and noun types (masculine, feminine, neuter, or plural). Choose a 30-card, 60-card, or full session. Each card supplies a correctly declined article and noun with a blank between them; type only the full adjective form, for example **guter** in **ein ___ Mann**. The case and noun type are shown, while the weak/mixed/strong pattern is revealed in the feedback. **View forms...** shows the selected adjective across all 48 combinations. A few adjectives use a contracted stem, such as **hohe** from *hoch* and **teure** from *teuer*. Noun phrases are grammar prompts and can be unusual in natural speech.

The checklists have search, **Select all**, **Clear all**, and a selected count. Selections are remembered separately for each language and mode. Exercises have on-screen character buttons, a card queue count, answer feedback, and a session summary of incorrect, skipped, and unseen cards. Answers must use the stored spelling, including Spanish accents, Russian `ё`, and German umlauts and `ß`. German noun answers also require capitals.

On macOS 27 with older Qt versions, selection lists use Qt's Fusion style so their checkboxes remain visible (QTBUG-150017). This only changes the checklist appearance.

Data comes from the top-level `language.yaml` and `spec.yaml`, bundled as Qt resources during the build. Rebuild after editing either YAML file. A lightweight reader handles the current fields; if the YAML structure changes, update the data readers too. Run `LangTrainer --check-data` to validate the bundled Spanish, Russian, and German decks, including German pronouns and adjectives. `python3 tools/generate_german_data.py` regenerates the German YAML block from its reviewed paradigms; run it from the repository root after editing that script, then rebuild.
